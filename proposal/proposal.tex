\section{Motivation and related work}
HireMind is our Senior recruitment platform. We investigate a compact CV--job fit classifier that could later provide one piece of evidence to its scoring workflow; it does not replace skill verification or recruiter decisions, and platform integration is future work. A small local model could reduce reliance on external API calls for matching and keep this step inside an organization, but we only claim what we measure: quality, storage, latency and memory.

\citet{gan} motivate automated resume screening. BERT \citep{bert} provides the architecture, and \citet{turc} show that pretrained compact students benefit from distillation: a 6-layer student's GLUE development meta-score rises from 82.8 to 84.4. \citet{hinton} introduce temperature-based distillation; on MNIST, a distilled small network makes 74 errors instead of 146. Q8BERT \citep{q8} shows that 8-bit quantization-aware training keeps MRPC F1 near FP32 (89.56 vs.\ 90.0), while post-training dynamic quantization loses more (87.88). \textbf{Gap:} these five sources study distillation and quantization separately; none asks whether a distillation gain in a 4.4M-parameter student survives \emph{post-training} 8-bit quantization that also covers the embeddings, which hold about 90\% of BERT-Tiny's weights (3.97M of 4.4M parameters). This matters: if quantization erases the gain, the cheaper hard-label model would be enough. We study this combination empirically on CV--job pairs, without claiming a new algorithm.

\section{Question and proposed approach}
\textbf{Research question:} Can teacher-guided training improve compact CV--job fit classification, and can post-training quantization cut storage while keeping that improvement?

Input is a CV and job description; output is \emph{No Fit}, \emph{Potential Fit} or \emph{Good Fit}. The student is Google's pretrained BERT-Tiny, \texttt{bert\_uncased\_L-2\_H-128\_A-2}, with a three-class head. A fine-tuned \texttt{bert-base-uncased} teacher (same three-class head) supplies logits.

\textbf{M0:} student trained with hard-label cross-entropy. \textbf{M1:} same architecture and initialization, trained with
\begin{equation}
L=0.5L_{CE}+0.5\,T^2\,\mathrm{KL}\big(\sigma(z_T/T)\,\Vert\,\sigma(z_S/T)\big),
\end{equation}
where $z$ are logits and $\sigma$ is softmax; the $T^2$ factor is implemented. \textbf{M2:} a CPU copy of M1 converted with PyTorch \texttt{quantize\_dynamic}: Linear layers use dynamic INT8 (\texttt{default\_dynamic\_qconfig}) and embeddings 8-bit weight-only (\texttt{float\_qparams\_weight\_only\_qconfig}); layer normalization stays FP32. M2 needs no extra training. Distillation is Part 1; quantizing its result is Part 2. All models share preprocessing and a 256-token limit; truncation frequency is reported.

\section{Hypotheses}
Let $F_i$ be mean test macro-F1 (0--100) over three seeds and $S_i$ serialized size.
\textbf{H1:} $F_1-F_0\geq1.0$ point.
\textbf{H2:} $S_2\leq0.40S_1$, $F_1-F_2\leq1.0$ point, and $F_2-F_0\geq0.5(F_1-F_0)$.
Targets are exploratory: 60\% is motivated by the embedding share, not by a pilot. H2 is fixed now and is not changed after the pilot. If H1 fails, H2's retention part is not confirmatory and only compression is reported.

\section{Data and fair experiments}
We use the instructor-approved resume--job-description fit dataset on Hugging Face,\footnote{\url{https://huggingface.co/datasets/cnamuangtoun/resume-job-description-fit}} pinned to revision \texttt{08978e2}: 8,000 pairs (6,241 train, 1,759 test), 643 CVs, 351 jobs, labels 4,000/2,000/2,000. CV and job IDs are SHA-256 hashes of whitespace-normalized text. Rows of the six conflicting pairs (12 rows) are dropped from whichever split holds them; for the one exact duplicate, a copy in training is dropped if the pair also appears in test, leaving 7,987 unique pairs. About 20\% of training jobs, grouped by job ID, form validation (seed 42), so no validation or test job appears in training; the test file is otherwise untouched. The audit script outputs the final train/validation/test counts, which are published with split indices and file hashes on GitHub before training; CV text is not redistributed. The official split shares 476 CVs but no jobs, so we measure \emph{unseen jobs with possibly familiar CVs}, not new candidates.

\textbf{Label validity.} Provenance, licence and labeling method are undocumented. Before training, both authors independently label 90 training pairs, sampled randomly within each class (30 per class, seed 42), using a written rubric on skills, experience and role match. We report Cohen's $\kappa$, agreement with dataset labels and per-class disagreements. This is a limited audit, not validation of all labels; test scores mean agreement with benchmark labels, not hiring validity.

\textbf{Training.} AdamW, effective batch 16, at most three epochs, equal update caps. M0 tries four learning rates; M1 tries two learning rates $\times$ $T\in\{2,4\}$, so both get four validation trials. Selected settings are rerun as three independent seeds (42--44) from the same pretrained start; we report all three paired differences and their mean $\pm$ s.d., without significance claims. The teacher is trained once on training data, selected on validation, and its cached logits are reused for every M1 run. All choices are frozen before the test set is opened.

\textbf{Measurement.} Macro-F1, accuracy, per-class scores and confusion matrices. The primary compression measure is serialized size: bytes of the model \texttt{state\_dict} saved with \texttt{torch.save}, excluding tokenizer and config, identically for M1 and M2. Latency and memory are separate empirical outcomes, not assumed consequences of quantization. Latency uses one pinned PyTorch version on one stated CPU, one thread, batch size 1, 256 tokens, 30 warm-up and 200 timed calls (median, p95), reporting model-only and tokenization-inclusive times separately, plus peak memory. MACs describe the operation graph, not realized speed.

\section{Feasibility and team plan}
No model has been trained. A Colab pilot on a small subset must pass: (1) Linear and Embedding modules convert; (2) M2 saves and reloads, with embedding weights still stored in 8-bit; (3) outputs are finite; (4) M2 agrees with M1 on at least 95\% of validation predictions. We also record serialized size, latency and memory. Our two-person team is approved by the instructor. Rawan leads data auditing and measurement; Hams leads training and export; both verify and write. Track: Technical Report. GitHub and Overleaf projects are shared with the instructor. Weeks (to be confirmed with the calendar): 10 labels and split; 11 pilot, teacher, M0; 12 M1; 13 M2 and profiling; 14 final evaluation; 15 report, presentation, poster.

\section{Risks and fallback}
If embedding quantization fails the pilot, H2 is reported as untestable for the planned M2; a Linear-only variant is then evaluated and reported separately as exploratory, with no success target. If the teacher does not beat M0 on validation, M1 still runs as planned and is reported; class-weighted training would be a separate, labelled exploratory run, never a replacement for M1. Memory limits: gradient accumulation. Label noise and truncation are reported as limitations. Predictions will not support autonomous hiring or fairness claims.
